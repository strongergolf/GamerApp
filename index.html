App References

The StrongerGolf vocabulary, worded to keep front-of-mind that impact is a 3D event: every variable has a Vertical and a Horizontal component of one 3D vector. Tap any card to expand deeper context and alternate launch-monitor names.

Definitions
Impact Plane VPlane · HPlane ↕ tap
The shape of the clubhead's motion through the bottom of the swing. VPlane = the "lie angle" of that motion (upright/flat, DTL). HPlane = its horizontal aim (overhead). Not to be confused with Path.

VPlane is the vertical tilt of the swing arc. A driver address produces a lower VPlane; a 9-iron a higher one. HPlane is the horizontal aim of that arc from overhead — distinct from HPath (the instantaneous travel direction at the ball). A "5SK" swing-plane matching drill targets HPlane specifically.

Trackman Swing Plane / Swing Direction  ·  FlightScope Swing Plane (V) / Swing Plane (H)  ·  Foresight GCQuad not reported (camera measures impact, not swing arc)  ·  Full Swing Kit Swing Plane / Swing Direction
3D Path VPath · HPath ↕ tap
Direction the clubhead is moving at impact. VPath = Angle of Attack (up+/down−). HPath = left/right of target line.

Ideal VPath: driver +2–5°, fairway woods ~0°, irons −2–5° (steeper for shorter clubs). HPath + HFace together set start direction: ball starts ≈ 75–85% toward HFace, then curves via HDiff. Out-to-in + open face = fade; in-to-out + closed face = draw. HPath alone does not determine curve — only HDiff does.

Trackman Attack Angle / Club Path  ·  FlightScope Club Attack / Club Strike  ·  Foresight GCQuad Attack Angle / Club Path  ·  Full Swing Kit Attack Angle / Club Path
3D Face VFace · HFace ↕ tap
Direction the face points at impact. VFace = Dynamic Loft (incl. shaft lean). HFace = open/closed vs target.

VFace drives launch height and spin. Forward shaft lean reduces VFace (deloft). HFace is the dominant ball-start influence — the ball starts ≈ 75–85% toward HFace at impact, not purely along HPath. A square face with an out-to-in path still starts the ball right-of-path. Closing the face causes a left start regardless of path.

Trackman Dynamic Loft / Face Angle  ·  FlightScope Dynamic Loft / Face Angle  ·  Foresight GCQuad Dynamic Loft / Face Angle  ·  Full Swing Kit Dynamic Loft / Face Angle
Spin Loft VDiff · HDiff ↕ tap
3D angle between Face and Path, along the D-plane. VDiff = VFace − VPath (drives launch & spin). HDiff = HFace − HPath (tilts the spin axis = curve).

Target VDiff by club: driver 10–14°, fairway woods 12–16°, irons 20–25°, wedges 20–35°. Higher VDiff = more spin + higher launch. HDiff tilts the spin axis — larger HDiff relative to VDiff = stronger side-curve. Watch-out: most monitors report only VDiff as "Spin Loft." True 3D Spin Loft = √(VDiff² + HDiff²).

Spin Axis ↕ tap
Tilt of the ball's spin axis vs the horizon — the curve. Left(−) / Right(+).

Left axis (−) = draw or hook. Right axis (+) = fade or slice. Rule of thumb: 5° at 2500 rpm = mild curve, 10° = moderate, 15°+ = significant. The D-plane model gives an estimated spin axis; contact location (gear effect) shifts the actual value away from the estimate — that gap is why dispersion ellipses widen at the edges of the face.

3D Launch VLaunch · HLaunch ↕ tap
Initial ball direction. Ball starts on the D-plane, 75–85% toward the Face (≈75% wedges, 85% driver), then curves off the path.

The 75–85% face-influence rule varies with loft: higher loft (more spin) means the ball starts closer to face direction. VLaunch is mainly VFace-determined; HLaunch is mainly HFace-determined. Launch direction ≠ target — account for expected curve when planning a shot start line. The D-plane geometry ensures the ball always starts on the D-plane.

Gear Effect — off-centre strike (RH; mirror for LH)

Why predicted ("estimated") spin axis diverges from actual: contact location bends the result. Tap any card for more.

Toe contact ↕ tap
Spin axis tilts left → more hook/draw spin.

The face CG is inward of the toe. A toe strike rotates the face closed around the CG, imparting draw/hook spin via friction. Most pronounced on driver (large face relative to shaft). On short irons the gear effect is smaller. If your actual trajectory draws more than the D-plane model predicts, check for toe contact in your miss tendency data.

Heel contact ↕ tap
Spin axis tilts right → more fade/slice spin.

Opposite of toe contact: the face rotates open around the CG, adding fade/slice spin. If actual trajectory fades more than the D-plane predicts — and face angle looks square — suspect a heel strike. Common cause: over-the-top move that shifts the contact heel-ward through impact.

High on face ↕ tap
Decreases spin rate and increases vertical launch.

High contact is above the face CG. The gear effect tilts the face upward, launching the ball higher with less spin — a "hot" launch. On driver, a slightly high strike is desirable: it often yields a positive smash-factor bonus and lower spin that gains carry distance. The Driver Optimizer in this app targets the upper-middle of the face.

Low on face ↕ tap
Increases spin rate and decreases vertical launch.

Low contact is below the face CG. The gear effect tilts the face downward, producing a lower, spinnier ball flight. With driver, low strikes are costly: high spin eats carry and exposes the shot to crosswind. On irons, low contact deepens the divot and exaggerates compression — which can be intentional for trajectory control in wind.
